# Scoring, risk and interpretation

## Version2 update

Current horizon-aware scoring and cost-aware sizing are defined in [the quantitative audit and formulas](QUANT_AUDIT.md). The legacy six-category composite and single-plan thresholds below are preserved for audit/reference and unit tests; they no longer supply the dashboard's main score or investment plan.

All scores are bounded 0–10 and describe evidence, not probabilities of profit. Default category weights: financial quality/growth 25%, valuation 20%, technical 20%, news 10%, macro 10%, risk/resilience 15%. The browser accepts percentages totaling exactly 100%; the API accepts fractions totaling 1.

Investment attractiveness = sum(available score × original weight) / sum(available original weight). Every original weighted contribution remains visible. Missing categories are not assigned neutral or favorable scores. The overall score is withheld below 60% available requested weight, or when the financial or technical score is absent. `observed_weight_score` is diagnostic, not a full rating. The displayed confidence is the sum of category confidence × original weight, multiplied by agreement = max(0.5, 1 − population standard deviation of available category scores / 10). Coverage and confidence are separate. Confidence is a heuristic evidence-quality measure, not a calibrated statistical probability.

Company quality = 70% financial profitability/growth/cash-generation/capital-efficiency + 30% statement resilience. If resilience is unavailable, quality uses available financial evidence with reduced quality confidence. Financial score excludes leverage, liquidity and interest coverage; those form the separate resilience score. Macro, price momentum, valuation and news cannot improve management/business quality.

Investment risk score combines statement resilience 50% with measured market risk 50%. Market risk is the average of annualized daily-close volatility and trailing available-window maximum drawdown subscores. Volatility reference knots: [10%,20%,35%,60%,100%] → [10,8,5,2,0]; drawdown magnitude [5%,15%,30%,50%,80%] → [10,8,5,2,0]. Linear interpolation between knots, end values clipped. Missing components reduce risk confidence. These thresholds are user-visible generic heuristics, not sector percentiles. Statement resilience uses different inputs from the financial quality category. Historical volatility is not scheduled event risk.

Source cache failure halves company category confidence and quarters quote-dependent confidence when quote evidence is stale. Quotes older than five calendar days, future dated quotes or a failed-refresh history cache are considered stale; this deliberately allows ordinary weekends but does not implement every exchange holiday. Annual/quarterly freshness is reported separately. The most recent dated price source is selected; an older metadata quote cannot override a newer daily session. Daily-bar timestamps identify sessions rather than exact last trades. During a regular session daily technical signals are marked provisional.

## Conditional plans

This release generates long-only research plans; it does not short securities or send orders. Default per-trade capital risk is 1%; the API bounds it at 5%. `capital` means the total capital allocated to this stock strategy, including an existing position. Existing shares consume both allocated capital and the modeled risk budget. Cost basis only computes unrealized P&L and never improves a stock score.

The nearest detected support below the current quote anchors entry. The zone extends ±0.15 ATR for short-term, ±0.25 ATR for swing and ±0.50 ATR for long-term, capped at the quote. Stop = min(support − 0.5 ATR, entry-zone high − k ATR), with k=2.5/2.0/1.5 for low/medium/high tolerance and an additional ATR for a long horizon. All levels are rounded before sizing (two decimals for prices >=1; six below1). Stops are floored at0.01; invalid or nonpositive-risk zones are rejected.

The first target is the nearest identified resistance above the quote; the next distinct resistance is the second target. Explicit DCF conservative/base estimates may supplement long-horizon targets. Missing second targets remain absent. Targets are never manufactured by adding an arbitrary required R multiple. Reward/risk = (first target − entry-zone high) / (entry-zone high − stop). Upside/downside percentages use the same entry reference. `calculation` discloses every input and equation.

Additional whole shares are the lower of affordable capital sizing and remaining risk-budget sizing, after existing holdings. Sizing uses the worst fill in the zone, before fees/taxes. A buy candidate requires overall >=6, confidence >=40%, a supportive trend, positive affordable size, first-target reward/risk at least the configured minimum (default2), and no high-impact event in the strategy's waiting window. An existing position uses Hold/Manage or Reduce/Sell candidate. Below3.5 is Avoid/Reduce; missing evidence is Insufficient Data; otherwise Wait. No favorable setup means no new suggested shares, even when illustrative levels are shown.

High-impact events within24h (72h for low tolerance) block new candidates under this strategy; calendar coverage limitations and broader horizon windows remain visible. Unknown calendar coverage never becomes a statement that there are no events. No schedule alone generates a buy/sell signal. Stops can gap through; actual loss may exceed the initial budget. A technical invalidation is not a guaranteed execution price or proof that the company thesis failed. A breakout alternative is shown only for an uptrend with adequate score/confidence, no blocking event, the next resistance within 2 ATR, and a second independent resistance meeting the reward/risk threshold. Trigger = first resistance + 0.1 ATR; stop = first resistance − ATR; target = second resistance. It requires a completed close above the trigger and relative volume ≥1.2, followed by a new size calculation. No shares are allocated to an unconfirmed alternative.

## Limitations

These equations are transparent decision rules, not trained predictive models. Industry peer distributions, comparable historical valuation snapshots, historical news availability, geographic revenue exposure and policy futures expectations are not manufactured. Specialized financial institutions require metrics that Yahoo may not supply; generic quality scoring abstains. See METHODOLOGY_ANALYSIS.md and SOURCES_CALENDAR.md for all underlying formulas and source constraints. No expected-return forecast or guaranteed intrinsic value is produced.
